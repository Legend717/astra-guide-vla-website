\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

In robotic manipulation, deciding what to do next often requires information from past observations. A robot may need to remember which container hides a target, count earlier events, or reproduce a demonstrated action sequence. RoboMME evaluates these capabilities through 16 tasks grouped into counting, permanence, reference, and imitation \citepending{robomme}. Solving these tasks requires both reasoning over relevant history and executing actions consistent with it.

Recent RoboMME studies use GPT-6 Astra (hereafter Astra), a vision-language reasoning model, in two distinct roles. Direct control asks Astra to generate robot motion targets from visual history, placing both task reasoning and action generation in the model and requiring repeated inference during execution \citepending{astra-direct}. A three-tier system instead uses Astra to plan subgoals for a trained vision-language-action model (VLA), with a separate learned monitor tracking their completion \citepending{astra-three-tier}. This division reduces Astra's involvement in routine motion but largely confines its role to planning. We explore how Astra can take a more active role in supervising VLA execution, particularly when to review progress and when to intervene.

Reviewing every short action sequence, or action chunk, incurs repeated inference cost even when the VLA is still executing the same subgoal. Longer intervals between reviews, however, can delay correction of a failed grasp or an incorrect motion. The appropriate level of supervision depends on the task stage, observed progress, and how the VLA has behaved in similar situations. Experience from earlier episodes can inform these decisions, provided it is revised when new outcomes reveal its limitations.

To address this trade-off, we present a self-evolving harness that uses accumulated execution experience to guide Astra's supervision of the official RoboMME GroundSG VLA (Figure~\ref{fig:teaser}). Given task history and current observations, Astra selects a subgoal and assigns a finite execution budget. For longer execution intervals, it can activate auxiliary monitors, such as gripper-state or directional-motion checks, to trigger review before the budget expires. The VLA continues to act on updated observations throughout this interval. At each review, Astra decides whether to continue execution, revise the subgoal, or intervene directly. Initialized with subtask-duration priors derived from the VLA training data, the harness accumulates and revises experience across episodes to guide subsequent supervision decisions without changing the VLA weights.

We evaluate the harness on all 16 RoboMME tasks, with 50 episodes per task. It achieves 97.1\% success across 800 episodes, reaches at least 90\% on every task, and executes 54.9\% of control steps without additional Astra review. Execution and experience traces show how prior outcomes lead to revised review intervals and more specific task interpretations. These results show that experience-guided supervision can achieve high task success while allowing the VLA to execute substantial portions of a task without repeated Astra review.
